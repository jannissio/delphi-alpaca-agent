# Delphi: a risk process first, a trading agent second

*One-page write-up for the lablab.ai x Alpaca AI Trading Agents Hackathon. Paper account ID: see submission form. Public repo: MIT.*

**We do not claim a statistically detectable edge. We claim a certified bound per trade, a risk process that behaved exactly as specified, and a ledger anyone can check.**

## What the evidence allowed us to build

Over 2.5 trading sessions no options strategy has a measurable expected return: the index variance risk premium is real but worth about 2-3 % a year (Carr & Wu 2009; Bakshi & Kapadia 2003) and "over the past 15 years, option alphas have become indistinguishable from zero" (Dew-Becker & Giglio 2025); same-day-expiry premium selling is roughly zero after costs (Almeida, Freire & Hizmeri 2025; Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda 2023), and Vilkov's 0DTE study, whose transaction-cost model the author corrected in August 2026 after half-spreads had been charged at 1/100 of their true size, now finds no structure with a materially positive net Sharpe ratio (condor bucket -0.96 to -2.67; we cite the corrected result). The measurable half of the index-option premium is earned overnight, not during the day (Muravyev & Ni 2020; Papagelis & Dotsis 2025), and Delphi never holds overnight. With three daily observations the Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio cannot reach 95 % at any performance level once returns are fat-tailed (Bailey & Lopez de Prado 2014), and the evidence ceiling is arithmetic: three perfect sessions at credit/width 0.20 cannot push an anytime-valid p-value below 0.51 (`docs/THEORY.md` section 9). So the deliverable is a small, defined-risk, fully explained book, a control system that a regulator would recognise, and an evaluation object that needs no statistical power.

## The Conformal Risk Control Condor

An iron condor is a short prediction interval for the session close, and its payout to the buyer is a bounded loss that falls with the interval's radius. We choose the radius by conformal risk control (Angelopoulos, Bates, Fisch, Lei & Schuster, ICLR 2024): on the trailing 250 sessions of SPY moves in implied-volatility units, the smallest radius whose corrected empirical payout ratio is at most 10 % of the wing. Under exchangeability of the scores this certifies, in finite samples, that the expected payout is at most 10 % of the wing (Theorem 3 in `docs/THEORY.md`); after every session, traded or not, an online level moves by a fixed step and may only tighten the radius, which yields a one-sided long-run bound with no distributional assumption at all (Rolling Risk Control, Feldman et al. 2023; Theorem 4). The market's price of the same interval is read off the quote: credit divided by wing is the integral of the risk-neutral survival function across the wing band (Breeden & Litzenberger 1978; Lemma 2, exact). The rule is one line with no free parameter: **sell only if credit / wing, read at the expected fill, is at least 0.10 + 0.05**, the certified payout plus the modelled round-trip cost of four legs; its corollary is that every trade is certified, in expectation and under the stated assumptions, not to lose after that modelled cost, and a trade on the gate boundary expects about its cost and no more. The certificate is marginal while the gate selects; that gap, the four papers that name it, and why the exact repair needs data we do not have are stated in Theorem 3, remark v. Coverage, the quantity research/G controlled first, is the special case in which the loss is an indicator; it is kept as the logged counterfactual, because most breaches are partial and the break-even inside-probability is 75.7 %, not 83 %. On 618 historical sessions the realised payout ratio is 0.079 against the 0.10 certificate, below the bound in every year (0.070 / 0.079 / 0.090), while the fixed 1.10x rule drifts 0.119 / 0.113 / 0.073. What history cannot show is whether the gate pays: no option prices exist on the basic plan, so the market's side of the ledger exists only live, and every live session writes it (beta, k, strikes, credit/wing, empirical payout, gap, decision, and what the fixed rule would have done). Kelly is computed and logged as an exhibit: its standard error is (1+b)/b times that of the hit probability, six at our odds, so the 2 % cap binds and the record shows it (Lemma 5). We audited the literature adversarially and the claim survived in a narrower form than we first wrote it: conformal prediction has been applied to option prices (Bastos 2024), to market-maker positions (Wisniewski, Lindsay & Lindsay 2020), to realised volatility from implied volatility (Canete 2023) and to trading decisions themselves (Lekeufack et al., ICRA 2024; Ryan 2026), so "the first trading decision from a conformal interval" is not a claim we make. What we could not find is any work that selects option strikes by conformal risk control, treats an option structure as the sale of a calibrated prediction interval, or gates the trade on the market's own Breeden-Litzenberger price of that interval; the ancestors of the comparison (Ait-Sahalia, Wang & Yared 2001; Constantinides, Jackwerth & Perrakis 2009; Faias & Santa-Clara 2017) all estimate P and none certifies it. The deterministic lemmas are machine-checked in Lean 4 (13 theorems, no sorry); option pricing has been formalised before (Ushakov & Berdinsky 2026; Coelho 2026; Echenim, Guiol & Peltier 2018) and we cite it, while a machine-checked risk-control lemma for a trading decision rule had not, as far as we and the audit could find; the probabilistic step is cited, not formalised.

## AI logic: the model decides categories, code decides numbers

The LLM (open-weight models on Featherless.ai) reads anonymised headlines, the macro calendar and the volatility term structure and returns **enums only**: VOL_REGIME, TREND, EVENT_RISK, a strategy family from a fixed menu, and a veto with a reason. Three calls must agree; disagreement is converted into NO_TRADE. Three votes from one model family are a self-consistency filter, not an independent ensemble (majority voting beats the strongest member in under 10 % of size-3 subsets, Kim 2026; frontier-model errors are correlated), so unanimity is an abstention trigger that raises the NO_TRADE rate, and the gate, not the vote, is the control. A second LLM pass, the critic, sees the finished, gate-validated order and may say PASS, REDUCE or BLOCK, never "larger". Tickers and dates are masked (Glasserman & Lin 2023; Chen, Kelly & Xiu); sampling is pinned on temperature, top_p, top_k and seed (Koviazin, Mudarisov, Polyachenko & State, IC-AIF 2026, show temperature alone leaves 0.98 bits of decision entropy); every prompt and response is hashed and logged, and a replay script reports per-field decision entropy in bits. Determinism is an assurance property, not a correctness property: the byte-identical-order test proves the deterministic decision path is reproducible from the logged inputs, not that the model would return the same enum (1,000 temperature-0 completions of one prompt have been measured to give 80 distinct outputs, because inference kernels are not batch-invariant). Deterministic code places the strikes, sizes from the max-loss budget, prices the package in ticks (Fu, Li & Musto, SEC: 0DTE spreads are 1-2 ticks; percent collars veto everything) and manages the exit.

**Trained on history, allowed only to brake, and honest about it.** A logistic regression fitted on 1,533 SPY sessions (2020-2026, with decades of S&P 500 and VIX data for the regime features) estimates, from what is known at the morning entry, the probability that the day ends inside the short strikes, and sets the size multiplier to 1, 0.5 or 0; it can never increase size. Disclosed in full: on the exact 10:30-to-close horizon the logit is worse than a constant (two year-blocks, no inference possible); on the close-to-close horizon with fifteen blocks it is the best single model. XGBoost, LightGBM, a logit+XGBoost average, TabPFN v2 and TabICL v2 were run under the identical expanding-window protocol (21 configurations, all reported in `research/H`): the tabular foundation models win only where nothing can be measured and lose, with bootstrap intervals excluding zero, where it can. Reporting that two 2025/26 foundation models did not beat twelve coefficients is a stronger claim than shipping one. The same history gives the random-entry Monte Carlo null (2.5 sessions, two contracts per session: median +259, P05 -503 dollars) against which the campaign P&L is reported as a percentile, not as alpha.

## Risk gates

Thirty controls derived from SEC Rule 15c3-5, MiFID II RTS 6, FINRA Notice 15-09 and the Knight Capital order (`research/C`, `config/risk_limits.yaml`, `agent/gates/engine.py`), plus gate 31, the coverage gate above. The ones that fired or mattered: session max loss 2 % of capital ($2,000) with a continuous Grossman-Zhou taper toward a 6 % campaign cap; defined risk only (every short has a bought wing in the same package order); per-order caps of $1,000 max loss and 5 contracts; message-rate and fill throttles; duplicate-order window; stale, crossed or drifted quotes rejected; Greek budgets on the whole book; buying-power check at 1.25x; entry windows by day (Wed 10:00-11:00 and 12:30-13:30 ET, Thu after ISM at 10:15, **Friday NO_TRADE because of non-farm payrolls at 08:30 ET**), pause windows around scheduled releases, no entry after 14:00; flatten by 15:15 ET with re-quoted limit orders, on liquidity grounds (quoted out-of-the-money 0DTE strikes thin from about 14:30, deep puts first; Cboe freezes the same-day variance input to VIX1D once fewer than 60 business minutes remain; Alpaca stops 0DTE opens at 15:30 and its pin-risk process has been reported to close only the short leg of a multi-leg position; the late-day momentum literature is two-sided and we do not rely on it); kill switch file checked every cycle, cancel-all under 5 s (it stops the agent from sending; it cannot unilaterally cancel at the venue, and we say so); daily loss kill on an independent code path; order-echo and position reconciliation every cycle, any mismatch halts new risk; append-only audit log with git and config hashes; immutable risk config outside the agent's writable scope; incident runbook written before the first cycle.

## Alpaca infrastructure

Trading API through alpaca-py for account, clock, option chain snapshots, multi-leg (mleg) limit orders and positions, and IEX bars for the conformal scores; the Alpaca CLI as the independent monitoring and reconciliation path (`scripts/status.sh`, JSON to disk); the Alpaca MCP server for operator inspection during development and the demo. Cboe delayed indices supply VIX, VIX3M and VIX1D. Paper trading is enforced at start-up. The mleg sign convention (negative limit = credit) was verified against the API reference, not the tutorial.

## What we report

The P-versus-Q ledger for every session (beta*, beta_t, n, k, strikes, credit/wing and its per-side split, the empirical payout ratio at the strikes, gap, decision, the fixed-rule counterfactual, and after the close the realised ratio, the payout ratio and the level updates); `scripts/reproduce.py` regenerates every number in this document and prints MATCH or MISMATCH; gate evaluations and rejections per gate; NO_TRADE reasons; fill rung (mid, -1 tick, -2 ticks, natural) and slippage versus the decision mid; ex-ante versus ex-post Greeks; time-to-flat; LLM latency, tokens, vote unanimity and decision entropy; the leakage audit (masked versus unmasked decision, re-identification rate); P&L per package against SPY over the same hours and its percentile in the Monte Carlo null; the term-structure-implied event variance for the NFP day (Dubinsky et al. 2019). Not reported, on purpose: Sharpe, win rate, annualised return. Configurations tried: two strategy geometries (fixed, conformal) and 21 regime-model configurations, all disclosed.
